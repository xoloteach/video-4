## Source Log & Psychological Mechanisms

1. **Festinger, L. (1954), _A Theory of Social Comparison Processes_, Human Relations.**
   Supports: Upward social comparison (evaluating oneself against perceived superior performers) frequently induces feelings of inferiority, inadequacy, and envy, especially in ambiguous or highlight-curated settings.

2. **Vogel et al. (2014), _Social comparison, social media, and self-esteem_, Psychology of Popular Media Culture.**
   Supports: Chronic exposure to curated online representations (e.g. 5 AM routines, highlight reels) leads to poorer state self-esteem and elevated feelings of being behind.

3. **Neff, K. D. (2003), _Self-Compassion: An Alternative Conceptualization of a Healthy Attitude Toward Oneself_, Self and Identity.**
   Supports: Self-criticism and contingent self-worth (treating self-worth as a score based on daily output) lead to emotional fragility, task aversion, and burnout. Self-compassion fosters resilience and sustainable habit formation.

4. **Curran, T., & Hill, A. P. (2019), _Perfectionism is increasing over time: A meta-analysis of birth cohort differences from 1989 to 2016_, Psychological Bulletin.**
   Supports: Socially prescribed and self-oriented perfectionism have spiked drastically, driven by competitive individualism and lifestyle optimization pressures.
